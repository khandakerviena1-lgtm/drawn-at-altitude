# Dora AI — generation prompt

Paste the block below into Dora. It is written to follow the assembled film
(`source/film/_assembly/drawn-at-altitude-film-v3.mp4`) beat for beat, and to
steer Dora's 3D toward paper and pigment rather than floating geometry — the
brief rules out "WebGL demo" and "3D gimmick" as non-negotiables.

Colour values below are **measured from the actual artwork**, not invented.

---

Build an immersive, scroll-driven 3D landing page for **Drawn at Altitude**, a
nine-day travel-sketching immersion in Ladakh, September 2027.

**The one governing idea:** the visitor moves *through a travel sketchbook*,
not through a website. Every three-dimensional effect must be a **paper,
drawing, or camera behaviour** — depth between watercolour layers, a page
corner lifting, parallax between a photographed valley and the study drawn
from it, a spread turning. Never floating geometry, never rotating shapes,
never particles, never a product showcase.

**Depth model — exactly three planes, everywhere on the site:**
1. BACK — photographic Ladakh: mountains, the Indus, the camp. Slightly
   desaturated, deepest, slowest parallax.
2. MIDDLE — warm ivory sketchbook paper with visible fibre and a soft gutter
   shadow. This is the working surface and the site's true background.
3. FRONT — the drawing: loose imperfect ink line, transparent watercolour
   washes with granulation, faint graphite construction marks, a very few
   handwritten notes.

**The signature move, used repeatedly:** a photograph and the watercolour
study of the *same place*, composed so their horizons and masses align.
Scroll brings one into the other so they register — the same valley, real and
drawn, coming into coincidence. This is the site's central idea; make it the
most memorable interaction.

**Palette** (mineral, never saturated): ivory paper `#EDDFC9`, sand `#D8C19D`,
madder red `#9F4638` used only as a single accent, Indus grey-green, mauve
mountain, ochre, olive, stone grey. Large areas of untouched ivory are
required, not optional.

**Typography, exactly three roles:** Bodoni Moda for large editorial
statements; Inter, small and quiet, for practical information; a handwritten
face for margin annotations only — never for buttons or navigation. On
photography, type is either a large editorial statement or a small sketchbook
annotation, never weak mid-sized text floating over an image.

**Scroll narrative, in order:**

1. **OPENING** — full-bleed, no navigation, no buttons. An open sketchbook
   page: a watercolour of a timber window looking onto a valley. The camera
   pushes extremely slowly into the painted mountain until paper grain and
   pigment granulation fill the frame and become briefly abstract, then
   resolves into a real photographic Ladakh range.
2. **THE CLOUD** — high-altitude cloud drifts across and swallows the real
   mountain completely; as it clears it uncovers a watercolour study of
   Thiksey monastery on cream paper. Navigation appears here for the first
   time, quietly. The cloud's white should resolve into the cream of the paper.
3. **ACROSS THE PAGE** — the camera travels up the same spread to a second
   study on the same sheet: the Indus River Camp on the river bank.
4. **THE RIVER** — cut to a photograph: three wicker chairs on the bank at
   golden hour, mountains behind. Scroll pans down to a sketchbook held open
   in a hand containing *the same three chairs* drawn. Annotation:
   `Indus River Camp · Ladakh · 2 Sept`.
5. **THE FIVE PRACTICES** — not five feature cards. Five visually experienced
   principles, each a different decision, each on paper:
   `01 See & Simplify` a real observation reduced to five masses, showing what
   is removed · `02 Line & Structure` thumbnail, graphite construction, then
   restrained ink, construction left visible · `03 Light & Colour` value study,
   small palette tests, one transparent wash · `04 Depth & Atmosphere` pale
   cool distance, defined middle, warm textured foreground ·
   `05 Tell the Journey` the parts recombined into one spread.
6. **THE BIRD** — a real photograph of a bluethroat perched on a sea buckthorn
   branch dissolves into a watercolour field study of the same bird in the same
   pose. Blue throat and orange band hold still through the change; only the
   medium changes.
7. **NINE DAYS** — a quiet two-column ledger of nine observations. No chart, no
   progress graphic, no staircase.
8. **THE LADAKH FOLIO** — the climax, and the slowest scene on the site. One
   double page assembles in the order the work is actually done: bare paper →
   graphite construction → ink → pigment washes → botany → handwritten notes →
   stillness. Hold on bare ivory early and hold again at the end where nothing
   moves at all. Every element a live layer; never one flattened image.
9. **ANASTASIIA** — Anastasiia Morozova, Natura Illustrata. A short field-note
   introduction beside a portrait.
10. **PRACTICAL** — Season September 2027 · Duration 9 days · Route Leh → Leh ·
    Group 8 guests · Rooms individual · Base The Indus River Camp · Price
    €5,000 per guest, land only. International flights separate.
11. **CLOSE** — "Draw your way through Ladakh." September 2027 · 9 days ·
    8 guests. One button: *Request a place*.

**Interaction rules:** motion is scroll-choreographed, never decorative and
never on hover. After every revelation, stillness — build in deliberate holds
where nothing moves. Ink is *drawn* along its path, not faded in. Washes bloom
outward from where the brush would have touched. Respect reduced-motion.

**Tone:** editorial rather than commercial, sensory rather than technological,
handmade rather than digitally decorative, observational rather than touristy.
Sophisticated without looking like a luxury hotel site. Immersive without
becoming a 3D demonstration. Quiet enough that the landscape feels powerful.

**Do not produce:** a travel brochure, a hotel site, a SaaS template, a WebGL
showcase, rotating 3D objects, particle fields, glassmorphism, neon or gradient
accents, stock photography, generic UI animation, hover gimmicks, progress
bars, or tourism copy laid over landscape photographs.
