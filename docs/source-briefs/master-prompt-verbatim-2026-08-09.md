DRAWN AT ALTITUDE
IMMERSIVE LANDING PAGE + OPENING FILM
Ladakh · Indian Himalaya · September 2027
India-Khan × Natura Illustrata

## CORE EXPERIENCE

Create an immersive editorial travel-sketching landing page whose opening is not a conventional website hero.

THE FILM IS THE HERO.

The visitor should not arrive on a white page with typography and then watch a video.

The visitor should immediately enter Anastasiia's visual experience of Ladakh.

The central narrative is:

SKETCH → REALITY → OBSERVATION → MATERIAL → PIGMENT → SKETCH → INTERFACE → LEARNING → JOURNEY

The entire experience must communicate:

We are not simply travelling to Ladakh.

We are learning how to SEE Ladakh.

The interface itself should reproduce the process of travel sketching.

The Indus River Camp is not presented primarily as a hotel.

It is the field of observation.

Mountains become studies of mass.
Windows become studies of perspective.
Water becomes studies of reflection.
Plants become studies of mark-making.
Light becomes colour.
Materials become pigment.
Experiences become pages in a sketchbook.

## CRITICAL UX PRINCIPLE

The landing page begins inside the film.

There must be NO visible conventional website interface during the first moments.

No dominant navbar.
No blank luxury hero.
No giant static typography.
No CTA button floating over the first frame.

The navigation appears only later: when the opening film has nearly completed, or when the user intentionally begins scrolling.

The transition between film and website must be imperceptible.

NEVER: VIDEO → FADE TO BLACK → WEBSITE

ALWAYS: FILM → FINAL SKETCHBOOK → SAME SKETCHBOOK BECOMES HTML INTERFACE.

The final frame of the opening film must match the first interactive state of the website pixel-for-pixel as closely as possible.

The viewer should initially believe the film is simply continuing.

Then scrolling causes elements of the sketchbook to respond.

The film has secretly become the interface.

## OPENING TIMING

0–2 SECONDS — No interface. Open physical sketchbook. Paper. Graphite. Micron. Transparent watercolour. Quiet natural atmosphere.

2–5 SECONDS — Camera slowly enters one painted Ladakh mountain. Sketch → paper grain → pigment granulation → geological texture → real mountain.

5–10 SECONDS — The real Indus River Camp landscape is revealed. Allow the real landscape to breathe. Do not immediately fill it with information. Minimal optional typography may appear very softly: DRAWN AT ALTITUDE / A Travel Sketching Retreat in Ladakh. Then the cinematic material journey begins.

## PRIMARY FILM CONCEPT

Create one continuous, dreamlike but photographically believable journey beginning inside an artist's travel sketchbook, entering the real Indus Valley, moving through the physical materials of Ladakh, and eventually returning to the sketchbook.

The journey expresses: OBSERVE → DRAW → ENTER THE PLACE → EXPERIENCE ITS MATERIALS → TURN MATERIAL INTO PIGMENT → DRAW AGAIN.

This must NOT feel like a montage of beautiful travel footage.

Every physical environment should appear to generate the next.

Use ORGANIC MATCH CUTS.

A recognisable material approaches the camera. It becomes macro. Then abstract. At the moment when the viewer is no longer completely certain what material they are seeing, another real material emerges.

The viewer's reaction should be: "I don't know exactly when one thing became another."

## SOURCE OF TRUTH

The supplied Indus River Camp footage is the visual authority.

Preserve: actual geography, actual mountains, actual river, actual architecture, actual rooms, actual windows, actual textiles, actual vegetation, actual fire, actual natural light.

Do not replace Ladakh with an invented AI Himalayan landscape.

The AI should connect existing physical realities rather than redesign them.

## THE MATERIAL CHAIN

SKETCHBOOK ↓ PAINTED MOUNTAIN ↓ WATERCOLOUR GRANULATION ↓ REAL MOUNTAIN ↓ ROCK ↓ MINERAL STRIATION ↓ TEXTILE ↓ WOVEN LINE ↓ RIVER RIPPLE ↓ INDUS ↓ REFLECTED SKY ↓ REAL SKY ↓ WINDOW ↓ VEGETATION ↓ WARM LIGHT ↓ FIRE ↓ BURNT SIENNA ↓ WATERCOLOR PIGMENT ↓ BRUSH ↓ PAPER ↓ FINAL LADAKH FOLIO.

## TRANSITION RULE

Every transition must approximately follow:

RECOGNISABLE SUBJECT → approach → macro → physical texture → temporary abstraction → visual correspondence → new material → reveal → NEW RECOGNISABLE SUBJECT.

Transition anchors can use: SHAPE, LINE, TEXTURE, COLOUR, LIGHT, CAMERA DIRECTION, MOTION, REFLECTION, OCCLUSION.

Do NOT use conventional crossfades as the primary transition mechanism.

## CAMERA LANGUAGE

Slow, controlled pushes. Very intimate macro photography. Occasional deliberate pull-backs. Physical foreground occlusion. Realistic lens breathing. Subtle handheld imperfection when appropriate. Natural motion blur. Shallow depth of field during material transitions. More depth of field when revealing the actual landscape.

The camera should feel operated by a human observer. Never like a digital 3D camera.

## STRICT VISUAL NEGATIVES

NO fantasy particles. NO magical dust. NO sparkles. NO liquid CGI morphing. NO melting objects. NO obvious digital warping. NO surreal Himalayan geography. NO invented architecture. NO hyper-saturated travel advertising. NO generic luxury-hotel commercial. NO rapid commercial montage. NO artificial camera orbit. NO aggressive zoom. NO fake drone movement inside a room. NO generic AI parallax. NO digitally animated watercolor magically drawing itself. NO floating notebook. NO obvious fake hand anatomy. NO huge blocks of tourism copy over landscapes. NO visual effect merely because an effect is possible.

## PRODUCTION METHOD — 6 CONTROLLED GENERATIONS

DO NOT ATTEMPT TO GENERATE THIS ENTIRE FILM AT ONCE.

The opening film must be produced as SIX SEPARATE CONTROLLED GENERATIONS.

Each generation has: one defined narrative objective, one controlled start frame, one controlled destination frame, a maximum of one or two material transformations, strict continuity with the previous clip.

WORKFLOW: GENERATION 01 → review → approve or regenerate. ONLY AFTER APPROVAL: GENERATION 02 → review → approve or regenerate. Then GEN 03 → VALIDATE. GEN 04 → VALIDATE. GEN 05 → VALIDATE. GEN 06 → VALIDATE.

Never continue simply because a clip has technically generated. A generation is complete only when visually approved.

When approved, extract its strongest final frame. That exact image becomes: the locked visual continuity reference, and where supported, the START FRAME of the following generation.

Preserve between clips: camera trajectory, scale, colour temperature, light direction, lens character, geography, texture, motion velocity.

The six clips must eventually feel as if they came from ONE CAMERA MOVEMENT.

## CURRENT EXECUTION RULE

CURRENT EXECUTION STATUS: CONTROLLED GENERATION 01 ONLY.

Generations 02–06 are included only so the system understands the eventual visual continuity. DO NOT generate them. DO NOT preview them. DO NOT combine them. DO NOT anticipate their transformations. STOP completely when Generation 01 reaches its required end frame.

## CONTROLLED GENERATION 01 — SKETCHBOOK → REAL LADAKH

START FRAME: An open physical travel sketchbook. The page represents the real view from Indus River Camp: wooden window, terrace, vegetation, Indus River, layered Ladakh mountains. It must look like genuine travel sketching. Warm ivory paper. Visible graphite construction. Loose imperfect Micron lines. Transparent watercolour. Irregular edges. Generous untouched negative space. No digital illustration aesthetic.

CAMERA: Almost still initially. Then begin an extremely slow physical push toward ONE mountain painted inside the sketch. The camera enters the sketch. The drawing itself does not animate. Do not make ink appear. Do not make paint spread.

Move closer until: landscape becomes paper + pigment + graphite + granulation. The macro becomes temporarily abstract.

Then establish a visual correspondence: paper grain → rock grain; mauve watercolour granulation → mauve mineral texture; painted shadow → geological shadow; Micron edge → natural mountain striation.

Almost imperceptibly: PAINTED MOUNTAIN becomes REAL MOUNTAIN.

NO CUT. NO obvious crossfade. NO dissolving paper. NO magical transformation.

END FRAME: Real photographic Ladakh mountain. The sketchbook has completely disappeared. The camera is still gently approaching one usable diagonal geological line.

STOP. WAIT FOR HUMAN APPROVAL.

## CONTROLLED GENERATION 02 — MOUNTAIN → ROCK → TEXTILE

ONLY EXECUTE AFTER GEN 01 APPROVAL. Use the approved final mountain frame from Generation 01. Continue exactly the same forward movement. Landscape becomes mountain detail. Mountain becomes rock. Rock becomes mineral. Mineral becomes almost abstract. Preserve: dusty mauve, ochre, stone grey, warm brown. Select one diagonal geological striation. Match this with a line inside a REAL Indus River Camp textile. Visual correspondence: geological line → woven line; mineral grain → fibre; repetition in strata → repetition in weaving. Maintain the same direction and speed. Slowly reveal the textile. END FRAME: Real textile macro. Individual fibres visible. One flowing woven line becomes the transition route for Gen 03. STOP. VALIDATE.

## CONTROLLED GENERATION 03 — TEXTILE → INDUS → SKY

Start from approved textile. Follow the flowing woven line. Approach. Fibres become abstract. Repeated lines begin to resemble ripples. Highlights become reflected sunlight. WOVEN RHYTHM becomes WATER RHYTHM. Reveal the actual Indus. Remain close to its surface. Realistic water physics. Natural light. Tiny ripples. Then follow one blue reflection. Blue increasingly fills the frame. A reflected cloud becomes visually aligned with a real cloud. REFLECTED SKY becomes REAL SKY. END FRAME: Real Ladakh sky. Quiet motion. Enough uncluttered sky to enable the next reveal. STOP. VALIDATE.

## CONTROLLED GENERATION 04 — SKY → WINDOW → VEGETATION

Begin from the approved sky. Hold briefly. Then pull backwards. One wooden edge appears. Then another. Gradually reveal: we are looking at the sky THROUGH the large wooden Indus River Camp window. Reveal: wood, room, sunlight, river, mountains. This is a spatial revelation. Not a cut to an interior. Then move laterally. Pass behind: curtain or wooden frame. Allow it to physically OCCLUDE the whole lens. Use that real darkness to conceal the transition. Emerge into: REAL VEGETATION. Extreme close-up. Leaves. Dry grasses. Flowers. Wind. Warm sunlight. END: one ochre/orange sunlit plant dominates the frame. STOP. VALIDATE.

## CONTROLLED GENERATION 05 — VEGETATION → FIRE → PIGMENT

Follow the warm ochre/orange plant highlight. Move closer. Plant becomes: colour, texture, movement, light. Match that motion and colour to REAL Indus River Camp fire. Do NOT show the plant catching fire. Only visual correspondence. Reveal fire. Approach flame. Frame becomes dominated by: ochre, orange, burnt sienna, brown, red. At extreme macro the flame is no longer immediately identifiable. Match burnt-sienna fire colour to: REAL WET WATERCOLOR PIGMENT on an artist's palette. Reveal water. Pigment granulation. Brush hairs. Natural reflected light. A real brush collects the pigment. END FRAME: Loaded brush leaves palette toward ivory paper. STOP. VALIDATE.

## CONTROLLED GENERATION 06 — PIGMENT → BRUSH → FINAL FOLIO

Begin with the approved loaded brush. Follow it. It touches warm ivory paper. For the first time, show an actual deliberate painting gesture. ONLY ONE BELIEVABLE STROKE. Do not magically create an entire finished drawing. Move close enough to see: water, paper fibres, pigment travelling, granulation, uneven transparent edge. Then slowly pull back. The stroke becomes one portion of a painted mountain. Continue pulling backwards. Reveal a completed travel-sketch double-page. It contains memories encountered during the film: mountain, Indus, window, vegetation, architectural fragment, small observations. The page must remain restrained. Graphite visible. Micron imperfect. Transparent watercolour. Large ivory negative spaces. Very few handwritten notes. Camera gradually becomes still. HOLD.

This FINAL FRAME must also be designed to become THE FIRST HTML FRAME OF THE LANDING PAGE. No fade to black. No end-card break. The film stops, but the image continues existing in the browser.

## FILM → INTERFACE TRANSITION

Use the exact final folio composition of Generation 06 as the website state. When video playback finishes: replace video with an identically aligned HTML/CSS visual state. The swap must not be perceptible. At this point: navigation may gently appear. When the viewer scrolls: the sketchbook begins responding. An annotation shifts. A small painted detail separates subtly. A page corner lifts slightly. A photographed observation may expand. The viewer discovers: THE FILM HAS BECOME INTERACTIVE.

## FIRST EDITORIAL MESSAGE

Do NOT immediately present: ABOUT THE RETREAT. Continue the conceptual narrative first. Introduce: YOU HAVE ENTERED THE LANDSCAPE. NOW LEARN TO SEE IT.

## INTERACTIVE LEARNING JOURNEY

The website now reveals five practices. They must NOT look like five generic feature cards. The user should experience each principle visually.

01 — SEE & SIMPLIFY: Start from a real Ladakh observation. Complex scene: mountain, river, chair, window, vegetation. Progressively reveal how an artist simplifies it into approximately five major masses. Show visual selection. Show what is removed. Teach: seeing before drawing.

02 — LINE & STRUCTURE: Transform the simplified observation into: thumbnail, graphite construction, horizon, perspective, proportions, preliminary architectural lines, then restrained Micron contour. Construction marks remain visible. No perfect vector line. No generic animated SVG mountain.

03 — LIGHT & COLOUR: Show: 3–5 value study, sun and shadow, small Ladakh palette tests, mineral blue, mauve/grey mountain, warm earth, olive, ochre. Then: transparent wash. Colour emerges from observation rather than decoration.

04 — DEPTH & ATMOSPHERE: Show extremely clearly: distant mountain: paler, cooler, softer, less detailed. middle distance: more defined. foreground: warmer, higher contrast, more texture. Include: river reflections, vegetation, atmospheric distance.

05 — TELL THE JOURNEY: Recombine observations. Do not merely create one realistic picture. Build a page: major landscape + architectural fragment + small observation + colour note + handwritten memory + negative space. Reveal the completed travel spread. This is the culmination: SEE, LINE, COLOUR, ATMOSPHERE, STORY.

## IMPORTANT PEDAGOGICAL VISUAL RULE

Do NOT show ten nearly identical versions of one sketch becoming gradually more coloured. Each stage must demonstrate a DIFFERENT DECISION. The process should visibly contain: thumbnail studies, construction marks, mistakes, corrections, graphite, Micron, value blocks, palette swatches, test washes, margin exercises, texture studies, annotations, final composition. It should feel like pages from a real masterclass. Not an infographic.

## THE LADAKH FOLIO

The folio is NOT merely a later section. The entire website has been building toward it. Its emotional meaning is: THIS IS WHAT YOU COULD LEAVE LADAKH WITH. Not simply photographs. A personal visual record created by observing the place.

## MICRO-MATERIAL

Throughout the experience, repeatedly use intimate physical details: paper fibres, graphite dust, ink, water, pigment, brush hairs, rock, wood, woven fibre, grass, flower, river reflection. The visual language alternates: LANDSCAPE SCALE ↔ HUMAN SCALE ↔ MACRO MATERIAL SCALE. Avoid remaining permanently at hotel/architecture/panorama scale.

## RHYTHM

Not everything must move. After major revelations, allow stillness. Landscape reveal: pause. Finished sketch: pause. A particularly beautiful light condition: pause. Motion gains meaning because stillness exists around it.

## PHOTO ↔ SKETCH PRINCIPLE

Never allow long stretches in which Ladakh becomes merely travel photography. Whenever appropriate, connect reality back to seeing: REALITY ↓ WHAT DO I NOTICE? ↓ WHAT DO I REMOVE? ↓ WHAT LINE MATTERS? ↓ WHERE IS THE LIGHT? ↓ WHAT ARE THE COLOURS? ↓ HOW DO I RECORD THIS MEMORY? This principle distinguishes the experience from a hotel or destination website.

## REMOVE FROM CURRENT JET 2 DIRECTION

REMOVE the empty white hero introduction. REMOVE the generic clean SVG mountain line. REMOVE the digital staircase/progress graph. REMOVE long sequences that read primarily as: "stay somewhere beautiful." REDUCE conventional: scroll → full photo → scroll → another full photo. Avoid generic UI animations. Avoid generic feature cards. Avoid excessive full-screen photography without artistic interpretation.

## TYPOGRAPHIC SYSTEM

Use only three clear roles: EDITORIAL SERIF for major storytelling and titles. CLEAN SANS SERIF for practical information. HANDWRITTEN STYLE only for authentic artistic observation and sketchbook annotations. No arbitrary fourth or fifth typographic language. Typography on photography must choose one clear role: either large editorial statement OR small sketchbook annotation. Avoid weak mid-sized text floating over expressive photographs.

## CONTENT ARCHITECTURE AFTER THE IMMERSIVE EXPERIENCE

ORDER: FULL-SCREEN FILM (Enter the sketch) ↓ INTERACTIVE SKETCHBOOK (Learn how to see) ↓ THE FIVE PRACTICES (See · Line · Colour · Atmosphere · Story) ↓ THE LADAKH FOLIO (What you will create) ↓ THE JOURNEY (Where you will go) ↓ ANASTASIIA (Who you will learn from) ↓ THE RETREAT (September 2027, 9 days, Indus Valley, practical information) ↓ JOIN THE JOURNEY.

## OVERALL DESIGN FEEL

Editorial rather than commercial. Sensory rather than technological. Handmade rather than digitally decorative. Observational rather than touristy. Sophisticated without looking like a luxury hotel website. Immersive without becoming a 3D gimmick. Quiet enough to make the landscape feel powerful.

The experience should communicate: LADAKH IS THE SUBJECT. ANASTASIIA TEACHES YOU HOW TO LOOK AT IT. THE SKETCHBOOK IS WHERE THE JOURNEY IS TRANSFORMED INTO MEMORY.
